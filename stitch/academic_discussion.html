Your professor is teaching a class on economics. Write a post responding to the professor's question. In your response, you should express and support your personal opinion, and make a contribution to the discussion in your own words.

Dr. Harrison

Professor of Economics

Next week we will be moving on to discuss alternative economic models. One concept that has gained significant traction in recent policy debates is Universal Basic Income (UBI). Under this system, all citizens receive a regular, unconditional sum of money from the government, regardless of their employment status or wealth.

Proponents argue it eradicates extreme poverty and provides a safety net in an era of increasing automation. Critics, however, contend it would be prohibitively expensive and disincentivize work.

I'd like to hear your thoughts. Do you believe implementing a Universal Basic Income is a viable and necessary step for modern economies, or are the risks too great?

Sarah M.

2 hours ago

I strongly believe UBI is necessary. With AI and automation replacing jobs at a rapid pace, traditional employment might not be available for everyone in the future. UBI provides a baseline of dignity. Furthermore, studies have shown that when people aren't stressed about basic survival, they actually invest more time in education and entrepreneurial activities, which benefits the economy long-term.

David C.

1 hour ago

I disagree with Sarah. While the intention is good, the economic reality of UBI is flawed. The taxes required to fund such a massive program would stifle business growth and burden the middle class. Moreover, giving out money unconditionally can lead to inflation—if everyone suddenly has more cash, prices for rent and goods will likely just increase, negating the benefit. We should focus on targeted welfare and job retraining instead.
